WHAT THE DATA SAY (written after the runs; nothing here was assumed in advance).

At the 25% lesion size the targeted lesion produced a specific deficit only in the 3 billion model: made-up-word accuracy fell from 0.71 to 0.58 (mean over 7 agents) while the size-matched random lesion left it at 0.73 and the opposite lesion raised it to 0.79; real-word accuracy stayed within 0.02 of baseline. The targeted lesion cost more than the random one in all 7 agents, and in 3 of them the difference exceeds the 95% paired trial-bootstrap interval computed on all 100 trials. In the 7 billion model the targeted lesion did not hurt: made-up-word accuracy went from 0.92 to 0.96 while real-word accuracy dropped by 0.02 to 0.08 in 6 of 7 agents, a small shift of answers toward "made-up" (3 to 7 answers out of 100 changed), not a loss of ability. In the 32 billion model no session changed more than 2 answers out of 100. After reset() every one of the 21 agents gave exactly its baseline answers.

The lesion-size sweep (greedy agent, 5% to 50% of units per layer) shows the same ordering. At 3 billion the targeted lesion is worse than random at 5% (0.72 vs 0.82) and 25% (0.62 vs 0.82); at 40% both lesions hurt (0.58 vs 0.54), and at 50% the random lesion makes the model answer "made-up" to almost everything (real-word accuracy 0.52), so its made-up-word score of 1.00 is a collapse, not an improvement. Specificity at 3 billion is therefore a small-to-medium-lesion phenomenon. At 7 billion and 32 billion made-up-word accuracy never fell below 0.88 and real-word accuracy stayed at 1.00 at every size, targeted or random, up to half of the units in all five layers.

The localizer is not the reason. The units it picks are at least as selective in the larger models (median |Cohen d| across the five layers: 0.28 to 0.49 at 3B, 0.36 to 0.54 at 7B, 0.42 to 0.70 at 32B). Larger models contain units that prefer made-up words just as clearly; the answer simply does not depend on them.

Our reading: in this family, on this task, the computation that tells a made-up word from a real one runs through a localizable quarter of five late layers in the 3 billion model, and is redundant with the rest of the network from 7 billion upward. That is the reverse of the pattern one might expect if lesionability emerged with scale. Limits: five layers at the same relative depth are a smaller share of a 64-layer network than of a 36-layer one; the larger models start closer to ceiling (though 0.90 to 0.94 leaves room for a drop that never came); agents of one size share weights, so the seven agents are repeats of one subject under different sampling, not independent subjects; one random draw per size; one task. A full account would vary depth and lesion site, and test a harder reading task.
